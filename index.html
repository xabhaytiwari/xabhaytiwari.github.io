Open Questions I'm Exploring

The Map vs. The World

"If you draw a map that includes every blade of grass, the map becomes as heavy and useless as the world itself."

Current AI models waste enormous computer power trying to predict every tiny pixel of a scene. What is the simplest outline an agent actually needs to make smart decisions? Can it learn what matters for its goals while staying happily blind to background noise?

Hidden Rules and Conservation

"In physical reality, energy can never be created or destroyed—it is always perfectly balanced."

Having worked on continuous differential equations (PINNs & the Deep Ritz Method), I want to bring physical rules into reinforcement learning. Instead of using artificial penalties when an agent wobbles, can we build mathematical guardrails directly into the decision-making loop so it cannot fail?

The Decisive Move

"A quiet pawn advance on move 12 wins you the chess game 50 moves later. But standard algorithms give all the praise to the final piece capture."

How can an agent look back in time and accurately find the genuine root cause of its victory, instead of relying on humans to manually design artificial reward breadcrumbs?
